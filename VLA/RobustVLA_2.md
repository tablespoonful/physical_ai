**005 Anonymous authors 006** Paper under double-blind review **007 008 009** ABSTRACT **010 011** In Vision–Language–Action (VLA) models, robustness to real-world perturbations **012** is critical for deployment. ## **036** 1 INTRODUCTION 

**037** 

**038 039** Vision–Language–Action (VLA) models are a class of robotic foundation models that enable flexible, general, and dexterous manipulation through vision–language inputs (Zhong et al., 2025; Sapkota **040** et al., 2025). Trained on diverse, internet-scale robot data, VLAs can perform cross-embodied, **041** general-purpose control in real-world settings (Kim et al., 2025; Black et al., 2024; Bjorck et al., **042** 2025). Despite these advances, VLAs remain vulnerable to a wide range of multi-modal uncertainties **043** encountered in practice, including those in observation ( _e_ . _g_ ., sensory noise, camera errors), action **044** ( _e_ . _g_ ., sensorimotor noise, unexpected disturbances), environment ( _e_ . _g_ ., external forces, distracting **045** objects), and language ( _e_ . _g_ ., synonymous or ambiguous instructions). 

**046** Recent work has begun to explore the robustness of VLAs, but efforts remain limited in scope. **047** VLATest (Wang et al., 2025) primarily evaluates VLA robustness against visual perturbations, **048** focusing on uncertainties in environment transitions and observations. For enhancing robustness, **049** BYOVLA (Hancock et al., 2025) mitigates irrelevant visual details by identifying, segmenting, and **050** inpainting them using large vision–language models, while GEVRM (Zhang et al., 2025) improves **051** robustness to common visual corruptions such as color jitter through model-based planning. However, **052** these methods are restricted to visual robustness, leaving their effectiveness against multi-modal **053** uncertainties untested. Moreover, both approaches rely heavily on external large models, leading to substantial computational overhead.

**098 Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Foundation Models** . Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models serve **099** as foundational systems for robotics, integrating vision, language, and control. While these models excel in general-purpose embodied decision-making tasks, their **106** robustness remains a significant concern. ## 4 ROBUSTVLA 

To counter multi-modal uncertainties, we propose RobustVLA, a fine-tuning framework to enhance robustness against uncertainties in VLA inputs and outputs. We take _π_ 0 with rectified flow matching as an example due to its popularity, while our method naturally extends to VLAs with general diffusion-based action head and autoregressive VLAs like OpenVLA.
