**005 Anonymous authors 006** Paper under double-blind review **007 008 009** ABSTRACT **010 011** A central objective of manipulation policy design is to enable robots to comprehend **012** human instructions and predict generalized actions in unstructured environments. **013** Recent autoregressive vision-language-action (VLA) approaches discretize actions **014** into bins to exploit the pretrained reasoning and generation paradigms of vision- **015** language models (VLMs). While these models achieve efficient and scalable **016** training, the discretization undermines the continuity required for precise control. In contrast, diffusion-based VLA methods incorporate an additional diffusion **017** head to predict continuous actions, but they rely solely on feature representations **018** extracted from the VLM, without leveraging the pretrained large language model **019** (LLM) as an expert for iterative action generation. To integrate the complementary **020** strengths of autoregressive and diffusion generation, we introduce HybridVLA, **021** which innovatively leverages a shared LLM backbone to perform iterative action **022** prediction through both paradigms. Specifically, a collaborative training recipe is **023** proposed, incorporating diffusion denoising into the next-token prediction process **024** and mitigating interference between the two generation paradigms. With this **025** recipe, we find these two action prediction methods not only reinforce each other **026** but also exhibit varying strengths across different scenarios. Therefore, we design **027** a collaborative action ensemble mechanism that adaptively fuses both predictions, leading to more robust control. HybridVLA outperforms previous state-of-the-art **028** VLA methods by 17% and 19% in mean success rate on simulation and real-world **029** tasks, respectively, while demonstrating generalization to unseen configurations. **030**

> **034** Developing intelligent robots capable of performing manipulation tasks demands robust poli- **035** cies (Driess et al., 2023; Huang et al., 2023). In dynamic and unstructured real-world environments, **036** such policies need to interpret human instructions and generalize across a wide range of complex **037** tasks. Building on this success, several studies have extended VLMs into vision-language-action (VLA) models, enabling them to **040** predict low-level action poses for robotic manipulation (Brohan et al., 2023; Kim et al., 2024). This **041** paradigm outlines a promising roadmap for building foundation models to facilitate generalist robots. **042**

While these **052** methods enable precise manipulation, the diffusion head lacks internet-scale pretraining and depends **053** solely on feature representations extracted from the VLM, without fully leveraging the powerful LLM backbone as an action expert for iterative generation. This motivates a collaborative action ensemble mechanism that adaptively fuses both predictions. **283** 

**284 Diffusion actions.** When generating diffusion actions, we append the special token _<_ BOD _>_ after **285** the preceding condition tokens to signal the start of the denoising process. We employ DDIM (Song **286** et al., 2020) with _n_ sampling steps, and find empirically that _n_ can be reduced to as low as 4 while **287** maintaining an optimal balance between performance and inference speed. As shown in the right **288** part of Figure 2, at each denoising step, only the current noisy sample is input to the LLM to predict the noise for the next step, and the token sequence does not retain any previous noise samples. **289** Each denoising step is treated as a reasoning iteration, allowing HybridVLA to progressively refine **290** diffusion-based action predictions by leveraging the LLM’s pretrained knowledge. In this way, we **291** enable a multi-step Markovian denoising process that aligns with the LLM’s next-token prediction **292** mechanism. After obtaining the denoised tokens, we use an MLP to map them to the action space. **293** To accelerate sampling, we introduce a KV cache design for the diffusion process. During the initial **294** step, the model processes the vision and language tokens, the denoising timestep, and the initial noise. **295** In subsequent steps, only the updated timestep and noisy actions are forwarded, while the cached **296** keys and values are reused. This approach reduces redundant computation and significantly enhances **297** inference efficiency for diffusion-based action generation. 

**298 Autoregressive actions.** As shown in Figure 2, the autoregressive generation begins after the special **299** token _<_ EOD _>_ . Unlike previous autoregressive VLA methods (Kim et al., 2024), HybridVLA’s **300** autoregressive generation additionally conditions on continuous action representations derived from **301** diffusion tokens. This yields superior manipulation performance over standalone autoregressive **302** paradigms without explicit continuous latent conditioning, as shown in the ablation study. **303 Ensembled actions.** After obtaining the two types of actions under our collaborative training recipe, **304** we empirically observe two phenomena. 1) Different action types demonstrate varying performance **305** across tasks and scenarios. 2) The confidence of autoregressive tokens serves as a reliable indicator **306** of action quality. In over 80% of successfully completed test samples, the average confidence of **307** autoregressive action tokens exceeds 0.96 (range [0, 1]). Therefore, as shown in Figure 2, we use **308** the mean confidence of autoregressive tokens ( _c_<sup>_ar_</sup> _t_ +1<sup>) to guide the action ensemble.If the confidence</sup> **309** exceeds _θ_ ( _θ_ = 0 _._ 96), we consider the autoregressive action ( _a_<sup>_ar_</sup> _t_ +1<sup>) sufficiently accurate and perform</sup> **310** an average operation with the diffusion action ( _a_<sup>_d_</sup> _t_ +1<sup>).Otherwise, we rely solely on the diffusion</sup> **311** action to control the robot. Further analysis of the confidence threshold can be found in Appendix C.2. **312**

## E FAILURE CASE ANALYSIS. 

Through extensive real-world experiments, we identify three primary failure categories that impact the performance of HybridVLA. The first category, **rotational prediction deviations** , is particularly evident in tasks requiring precise rotation control, such as _Pour water_ and _Place bottle at rack_ . These failures include accumulated errors in multi-step rotational movements and incorrect rotation angles when interacting with target objects. The second category pertains to pose predictions that exceed the robot’s **degree of freedom limits** . The model sometimes predicts poses beyond the mechanical constraints of the Fr3 arm or AgileX dual-arm robot, generates target positions that fall outside the workspace boundaries, or produces kinematically infeasible configurations during complex transitions. The third category involves failures in **dual-arm coordination** , where both arms must collaborate to complete a task. Since the model predicts each arm’s actions based on the current object state, any interaction by one arm can alter the object’s state, potentially invalidating the previously predicted action of the other arm.
