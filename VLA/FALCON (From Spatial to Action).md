# ABSTRACT 

Existing vision-language-action (VLA) models act in 3D real-world but are typically built on 2D encoders, leaving a spatial reasoning gap that limits generalization and adaptability. Recent 3D integration techniques for VLAs either require specialized sensors and transfer poorly across modalities, or inject weak cues that lack geometry and degrade vision-language alignment. In this work, we introduce **FALCON (From Spatial to Action)** , a novel paradigm that injects rich 3D spatial tokens into the action head. FALCON leverages spatial foundation models to deliver strong geometric priors from RGB alone, and includes an _Embodied Spatial Model_ that can optionally fuse depth, or pose for higher fidelity when available, without retraining or architectural changes. To preserve language reasoning, spatial tokens are consumed by a _Spatial-Enhanced Action Head_ rather than being concatenated into the vision-language backbone. These designs enable FALCON to address limitations in spatial representation, modality transferability, and alignment. In comprehensive evaluations across three simulation benchmarks and eleven real-world tasks, our proposed FALCON achieves state-of-the-art performance, consistently surpasses competitive baselines, and remains robust under clutter, spatial-prompt conditioning, and variations in object scale and height. Code will be released publicly.

# 1 INTRODUCTION 

### **055** 

**056** 

**057** Recent advances in vision-language-action models (VLAs) have significantly advanced the pursuit of generalist robotics, enabling robots to interpret natural language instructions and execute intricate **058** action sequences (Brohan et al., 2023; Kim et al., 2024; Black et al., 2024; Li et al., 2024a). Most **059** advanced VLAs are built on 2D foundation models like Vision Language Models (VLMs) (Liu **060** et al., 2025; Shao et al., 2025; Alayrac et al., 2022; Radford et al., 2021; Peng et al., 2023; Liu **061** et al., 2023), aligning 2D images with text and leveraging the strong language understanding of the **062** Large Language Models (LLMs) for information processing. This design provides strong semantic **063** understanding and supports manipulation tasks conditioned on language and camera inputs. 

**063 064** 

However, while VLMs operate purely in the 2D domain, VLAs must interact with the 3D physical **065** world. This discrepancy results in a critical gap: current VLAs lack reliable 3D spatial understand- **066** ing, leading to persistent challenges in generalization and adaptability. Specifically, the absence of **067** explicit 3D awareness causes VLAs to struggle in scenarios that require reasoning about geometry, **068** depth, or spatial relations. _First_ , they exhibit limited generalization, failing to transfer robustly to **069** novel scenes, backgrounds, or object variations (Ze et al., 2025). _Second_ , they lack adaptability to **070** environmental changes, such as height variations or object scale differences (Li et al., 2025). These **071** limitations now form a major bottleneck in developing reliable generalist robot policies. 

**072** 

**072** To address this gap, recent works incorporate 3D information into VLAs (Li et al., 2025; Sun et al., **073** 2025; Zhen et al., 2024), most often by providing explicit 3D inputs (e.g., point clouds or depth **074** maps). While effective under ideal conditions, these methods suffer from low modality transferabil- **075** ity— **the ability to function and improve under different input modalities (RGB-only, RGB-D, 076 point clouds, camera pose) without retraining or collapsing.** This stems from two fundamen- **077** tal issues. First, acquiring high-quality 3D inputs requires specialized sensors that are expensive **078** and difficult to deploy in practice. Second, many large-scale manipulation datasets (e.g., Open X- **079** Embodiment dataset (O’Neill et al., 2024)) lack aligned 3D annotations, limiting scalability. As **080** a result, such methods are tied to specific input modalities and break down when those inputs are unavailable. 

**081** 

**082** An alternative direction introduces weak 3D cues, e.g., pseudo-depth estimates (like ZoeDepth (Bhat **083** et al., 2023)) or learnable spatial embeddings (Qu et al., 2025). However, these approaches face **084** three fundamental limitations. **(1) Limited spatial representation.** The learnable spatial embed- **085** dings provide only weak geometric signals within the high-dimensional space of LLMs, failing to **086** capture robust 3D priors necessary for tasks like reasoning about height differences or object sizes in grasping. **(2) Lack of modality transferability.** While encoding some 3D cues, they cannot **087** effectively exploit higher-quality 3D inputs when available. **(3) Challenges in alignment.** Concate- **088** nating spatial embeddings with text tokens risks disrupting the original vision–language alignment. **089** The scarcity of 3D data makes it difficult to properly align modalities, causing embedding drift **090** that degrades zero-shot generalization, especially in tasks requiring high-level reasoning like spatial **091** prompts (Qu et al., 2025). 

**092 Contribution.** We propose **FALCON** (From Spatial to Action), a novel paradigm that integrates **093** richer and more representative 3D spatial tokens into VLAs through an improved injection scheme. 

**094** 

**095** To solve limitation (1) i.e., weak 3D spatial representation, we leverage insights from spatial foun- **096** dation models that encode scenes into token sequences for holistic 3D reconstruction. FALCON **097** adopts broader and richer tokens from these foundation models, and delivers comprehensive spatial information from RGB signals alone, improving robustness when explicit 3D inputs are absent. 

**098** 

**099** For limitation (2) of poor modality transferability, we introduce an Embodied Spatial Model that **100** can optionally integrate extra 3D modalities (e.g., depth, poses). Unlike prior methods that require **101** specific 3D inputs (Li et al., 2025; Sun et al., 2025; Zhen et al., 2024), our design is flexible: when **102** RGB-D cameras or calibrated scenes are available, the model gains additional accuracy; when not, **103** it still performs effectively with RGB-only input. This optional pathway substantially improves transferability by flexibly utilizing 3D signals from sensors without requiring architectural changes. 

**104** 

**105** To overcome limitation (3) of alignment challenges, we draw inspiration from the brain’s division **106** of labor. The VLM (cerebrum) handles high-level reasoning and semantics, while the action head **107** (cerebellum) manages fine-grained motor control and sensorimotor integration (Rochefort et al., 2011; Figure, 2024). Motivated by this, we design a Spatial-Enhanced Action Head that directly

While VLAs (Brohan et al., 2023; Kim et al., **514** 2024; Li et al., 2024b;a; Cheang et al., 2024; Black et al., 2024; Kim et al., 2025; Cheang et al., **515** 2025; Zhang et al., 2025) have advanced generalist robotics by leveraging pre-trained VLMs, their **516** 2D nature limits spatial reasoning. Recent works address this by integrating 3D cues through two **517** main approaches: (1) explicit 3D inputs like point clouds (Li et al., 2025; Sun et al., 2025), which require specific sensors and lack modality transferability; or (2) embedding 3D features into VLM **518** input space (Zhen et al., 2024; Qu et al., 2025), which often disrupts pre-trained alignments and **519** requires costly retraining. FALCON overcomes these limitations by decoupling spatial processing **520** from the VLM, preserving semantic integrity while ensuring strong modality transferability. **521 522 Spatial Foundation Models** . Recent advancements in deep learning have introduced novel alter- **523** natives to traditional SfM methods. Recently, VGGT (Wang et al., 2025a) proposes a multi-view architecture that processes multiple images simultaneously, moving beyond pairwise processing to **524** improve reconstruction consistency and robustness. However, their integration into generalist robot **525** policies remains challenging, often requiring complex feature alignment or suffering from informa- **526** tion loss when adapted to action spaces. See comprehensive review of related works in Appendix A.2 **527 528** 5 CONCLUSIONS **529 530 531** In this work, we introduce FALCON, a vision-language-action model that augments generalist robot policies with robust 3D spatial understanding. While demonstrating impressive instruction-following capabilities, these meth- **719** ods operate primarily in the 2D domain, lacking explicit mechanisms for 3D geometric perception, **720** which is a critical limitation in tasks requiring precise spatial understanding. To address this, recent **721** works have integrated 3D perceptual cues into policy learning. One line of research directly con- **722** sumes explicit 3D representations like point clouds for action prediction, as seen in PointVLA (Li **723** et al., 2025) and GeoVLA (Sun et al., 2025). While enhancing geometric awareness, these meth- **724** ods rely on explicit 3D inputs and specific sensor configurations, limiting modality transferability when such inputs are unavailable. An alternative strategy embeds 3D features into the VLM’s input **725** space, exemplified by 3D-VLA (Zhen et al., 2024), SpatialVLA (Qu et al., 2025), and Evo-0 (Lin **726** et al., 2025), but this often disrupts pre-trained vision-language alignment, necessitating costly em- **727** bodied instruction tuning to recover performance. FALCON overcomes these issues by decoupling **728** spatial processing from the VLM, preserving its semantic integrity while achieving strong modality **729** transferability and robust performance across diverse sensory conditions, reducing dependency on **730** specialized sensor setups when deploy in the real-world. **731 732** A.2.2 SPATIAL FOUNDATION MODELS **733 734** Recent advancements in deep learning have introduced novel alternatives to traditional SfM meth- **735** ods. DUSt3R (Wang et al., 2024) represents a significant deviation from conventional SfM pipelines by predicting point clouds from image pairs without relying on geometric constraints or inductive **736** biases. Unlike traditional SfM, which depends on keypoint matching and geometric optimization, **737** DUSt3R generates predictions in a shared coordinate frame, enabling robust reconstruction across **738** diverse scenes. This approach addresses several challenges inherent in classical methods, such as **739** sensitivity to initialization and sparse correspondences. Building on this paradigm, several works **740** have proposed variations with distinct architectural innovations. MASt3R (Leroy et al., 2024) im- **741** proves the estimation of the pixel-wise correspondence between image pairs, strengthening the effi- **742** cacy of unconstrained feed-forward models for SfM tasks. CUT3R (Wang et al., 2025b) introduces **743** a recurrent formulation of DUSt3R, achieving computational efficiency at the expense of marginal **744** accuracy degradation. More recently, VGGT (Wang et al., 2025a) proposes a multi-view architec- **745** ture that processes multiple images simultaneously, moving beyond pairwise processing to improve **746** reconstruction consistency and robustness. However, their integration into generalist robot policies remains challenging, often requiring complex feature alignment or suffering from information loss **747** when adapted to action spaces. **748 749** A.3 TRAINING PARADIGM **750 751** A.3.1 FALCON TRAINING PARADIGM **752 753** Rather than incorporating spatial information during pre-training, which would significantly in- **754** crease computational cost and complicate optimization, we adopt a post-training approach that **755** preserves the original pre-training efficiency while enabling efficient integration of 3D geometric cues.

## A.7.3 DATA EFFICIENCY SUBSTANTIATION 

we have conducted additional experiments to provide a more comprehensive analysis of FALCON’s few-shot efficiency. We evaluated two few-shot adaptation tasks: (a) _stack blue block on red block_ and (b) _open drawer and place bread_ , under varying numbers of training demonstrations (1, 5, 10, 20), while keeping all other conditions identical and using only RGB inputs. The baseline model is RoboVLM (Li et al., 2024a) and learning curves are provided in Fig. 12, which clearly demonstrate FALCON’s superior data efficiency with consistent advantage across all data regimes. 

In the extremely low-data regime (only 1 demo), FALCON achieves 10%-20% success rates while RoboVLM fails completely (0%) on the stacking task. As demonstration numbers increase, FALCON maintains a consistent performance advantage, achieving a twofold improvement in the success rate (80%) than RoboVLM (40%) with 20 demos on the stacking task, which substantiates FALCON’s integration of geometric priors significantly enhances its ability to learn from limited samples. Moreover, for the sequential task ( _open drawer and place bread_ ), FALCON reaches perfect performance (100%) with just 20 demos, while RoboVLM plateaus at 80%, highlighting our method’s superior data efficiency in learning complex manipulation task.

## A.7.4 ESM ROBUSTNESS EVALUATION 

We conducted an ablation study by adding Gaussian noise to the injected depth condition during zero-shot monocular depth estimation on CALVIN benchmark. The results are shown in Fig. 13. When adding 10%–30% noise, the ESM performance with additional depth inputs drops from 99.79% to 77.96%, and the performance without depth inputs mantains the same. This indicates that injecting noise into the depth condition during test-time makes the depth prediction task more challenging and further degrades the overall performance of the network. 

## A.7.5 PARAMETER SWEEP ON BERNOULLI PROBABILITIES 

The _p_ in _b_ d _, b_ p _∼_ Bernoulli( _p_ ) is a training hyperparameter that specifies the probability of injecting depth/pose conditions during ESM training. This stochastic conditioning strategy enables robust scene reconstruction under both RGB-only and multi-modal inputs at inference time. 

We conducted an ablation study by sweeping _p_ across 50%, 66% (our standard setting), 80%, 90% and evaluating zero-shot monocular depth estimation performance on CALVIN. As shown in Fig. 14, _p_ = 66% yields the best performance trade-off between the with-depth and without-depth configurations. Moreover, under RGB-only inference, performance shows only a slight decrease (from 91.02% to 87.48%) as _p_ increases from 50% to 90%, demonstrating a clear but modest negative correlation. These results indicate that while higher _p_ values slightly increase depth-conditioned performance, ESM’s RGB-only inference remains relatively stable across different _p_ values.
