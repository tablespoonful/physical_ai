# dVLA: DIFFUSION VISION-LANGUAGE-ACTION MODEL WITH MULTIMODAL CHAIN-OF-THOUGHT

## ABSTRACT 

Vision-language-action (VLA) models have emerged as the next-generation framework in robotics, integrating visual perception, language reasoning, and robotic control into unified systems. In this paper, we present **dVLA** , a diffusion vision-language-action model with multimodal chain-of-thought. The dVLA optimizes visual reasoning, language comprehension, and robotic actions simultaneously through a unified diffusion-based objective. By harmonizing these modalities into a single cohesive framework, dVLA facilitates more effective crossmodal reasoning, enabling the model to generalize to novel instructions and objects. To ensure practical viability, we also integrate model acceleration methods that substantially decrease robot response times. Extensive evaluations in both simulation and the real world confirm that dVLA significantly outperforms current discrete and continuous VLA models, highlighting the potential of diffusion language model (DLM) based frameworks for robotics.

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

**027 028** Vision-language-action (VLA) models have emerged as the next-generation framework in robotics, integrating visual perception, language reasoning, and robotic control into unified systems (Black **029** et al., 2024; Brohan et al., 2023; Kim et al., 2024; Hu et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2025a; Intelligence **030** et al., 2025; Kim et al., 2025; Team et al., 2025; Bjorck et al., 2025; Zhao et al., 2025a;b; Zhen et al., **031** 2024; Wen et al., 2025a;b; Zhou et al., 2025b; Wen et al., 2024). The development of VLA models **032** has undergone two stages of evolution. In the first stage, a pre-trained vision–language backbone is **033** used purely as a feature extractor, and the extracted features are mapped directly to robot actions. As **034** vanilla VLA architectures proved inadequate for open-world instruction following and long-horizon **035** tasks, a second-stage training paradigm co-trains on image–text data alongside action trajectories **036** to preserve knowledge from the pre-trained VLM and, when necessary, to predict both sub-step **037** reasoning and robot actions (Zhou et al., 2025b;a; Intelligence et al.; Driess et al., 2025). The **038** sub-step reasoning, often referred to as Chain-of-Thought, grounds high-level instructions into low- **039** level sub-steps, thereby offering improved guidance for action prediction. Recent works have also incorporated image generation capabilities into VLAs, enabling the prediction of subsequent images **040** before generating actions, which is a visual form of Chain-of-Thought Zhao et al. (2025a); Cen et al. **041** (2025). Leveraging images as intermediate reasoning steps offers a more detailed description of the **042** next movement. Such approaches have demonstrated remarkable capabilities, enabling models to **043** generalize to novel environments, adapt to new objects, and even complete tasks requiring complex **044** reasoning, such as mathematical puzzle games (Zhou et al., 2025a; Zhao et al., 2025a). **045** Despite their promise, these models face several limitations. First, co-training visual-text data along- **046** side robotic action data, each with distinct objectives, often results in gradient conflicts. Specifically, **047** the gradients that enhance knowledge preservation and scene understanding may interfere with the **048** model’s ability to effectively learn robot actions, even when a separate module is dedicated to this **049** task. Second, integrating image generation into auto-regressive Vision-Language Models (VLMs) **050** is challenging due to the fundamental gap between training objectives and model architectures, **051** which makes harmonizing multi-modal generation and understanding difficult. Consequently, VLAs **052** struggle to fully exploit knowledge across all modalities, limiting their ability to capture the under- **053** lying physical laws that connect actions and generated images, even when equipped with an explicit Chain-of-Thought.

**054** To address these challenges, we propose dVLA, a framework that jointly optimizes visual reasoning, **055** image generation, and robotic manipulation under a unified diffusion-based objective. dVLA builds **056** on MMaDA (Yang et al., 2025), an advanced model in discrete diffusion language models that **057** unifies multimodal understanding and generation through a consistent discretization strategy, em- **058** ploying modality-specific tokenizers. To extend this foundation to actions, we adopt FAST (Pertsch **059** et al., 2025) to encode action sequences into compact discrete tokens, enabling dVLA to leverage **060** pretrained visual–textual knowledge for generating executable actions. However, simply discretiz- **061** ing actions and applying a unified training objective is insufficient. Such an approach exploits only **062** MMaDA’s multimodal understanding capabilities while neglecting its core strength—multimodal **063** generation. To overcome this limitation, we introduce a multimodal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) training paradigm, in which dVLA is required to simultaneously generate subgoal images (visual CoT), **064** textual reasoning, and action sequences. Concretely, during training we randomly mask tokens not **065** only from actions but also from subgoal images and textual reasoning, and the model is required to **066** reconstruct them across all available modalities. This design encourages dVLA to learn a shared pa- **067** rameter space, ensuring strong consistency between predicted subgoal images and actual execution **068** outcomes. Empirically, we observe that dVLA can even forecast failed execution images that pre- **069** cisely match real-world failures, suggesting that it learns not just to generate fixed sub-goal images **070** but also to capture the underlying physical laws governing action and perception. **071** In this paper, we conduct a comprehensive evaluation of dVLA through rigorous experimental anal- **072** ysis. On the LIBERO benchmark, dVLA achieves an average success rate of 96.4%, consistently **073** outperforms both discrete and continuous action policies, and achieves state-of-the-art performance. **074** We further validate our approach on a real Franka robot across a wide range of tasks, including the **075** challenging bin-picking task, which requires multi-step planning to complete. The results demon- **076** strate dVLA’s superior ability to handle the complexities of real-world scenarios, highlighting its **077** potential to significantly advance the capabilities of vision-language-action robotic systems. Since **078** multimodal CoT prediction increases inference cost, we introduce two acceleration strategies: prefix **079** attention mask and KV caching. These optimizations yield up to _∼_ 2 _×_ speedup in both real-world **080** tasks and the LIBERO benchmark, with only marginal performance degradation. **081**
