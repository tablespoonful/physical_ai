**005 Anonymous authors 006** Paper under double-blind review **007 008 009** ABSTRACT **010 011** To operate effectively in the real world, robots should integrate multimodal rea- **012** soning with precise action generation. However, existing vision-language-action **013** (VLA) models often sacrifice one for the other, narrow their abilities to task-specific **014** manipulation data, and suffer catastrophic forgetting of pre-trained vision-language **015** capabilities. To bridge this gap, we introduce **InstructVLA** , an end-to-end VLA **016** model that preserves the flexible reasoning of large vision-language models (VLMs) **017** while delivering leading manipulation performance with the help of embodied rea- **018** soning. InstructVLA introduces a novel training paradigm, _Vision-Language-Action Instruction Tuning (VLA-IT)_ , which employs multimodal training with mixture-of- **019** experts adaptation to jointly optimize embodied reasoning and action generation **020** on both standard VLM corpora and a curated 650K-sample VLA-IT dataset. **033** Large-scale pretraining has produced versatile foundation models in computer vision (CV) (Oquab **034** et al., 2023; Radford et al., 2021) and natural language processing (NLP) (Bai et al., 2023; Touvron **035** et al., 2023). Unlike prior manipulation benchmarks that primarily **304** focus on atomic actions or low-level control, SimplerEnv-Instruct captures two essential yet un- **305** derexplored abilities: (1) policy generalization to linguistic and visual diversity, and (2) contextual **306** reasoning in situated environments, evaluated in the _situated reasoning_ suite. **307 Task creation.** We remove trivial cases and design novel tasks requiring genuine generalization **308** rather than memorization. Novel objects and instructions are strictly out-of-distribution from the **309** originals, and all tasks are cross-validated by three annotators for clarity and consistency. In total, we **310** curated 80 tasks with 1.1K trials, about one third the size of SimplerEnv, keeping evaluation practical. **311 312** • **Task aggregation.** (50 tasks; examples shown in Figure 4, left). This suite assesses a model’s **313** ability to consistently interpret and execute core tasks based on both instructions and environmental **314** context, despite variations in visual or linguistic forms. Tasks cover phenomena such as novel **315** verbs, multilingual expressions, diverse object references, sentence rephrasings, and OOD objects. **316** • **Situated reasoning.** (30 tasks; examples shown in Figure 4, right). Beyond _task aggregation_ , **317** this suite evaluates a model’s ability to reason over contextual cues or indirect instructions and to **318** decompose commands into sub-goals. For example, “I want to clean the table. Pick a suitable tool **319** for me.” requires selecting the correct object (e.g., a sponge) from context. **320 321** Together, by leveraging the large-scale real-world training dataset, **SimplerEnv-Instruct** provides a **322** reproducible benchmark that evaluates VLA generalization to unseen tasks. It achieves an affordable **323** evaluation cost while systematically probing both task generalization and reasoning, filling a critical gap in VLA evaluation with a diagnostic, human-interpretable, and standardized benchmark.

In contrast, InstructVLA extends this idea by focusing **1480** on preserving the multimodal knowledge of VLMs and bridging high-level human instructions with **1481** low-level manipulation skills, enabling generalization to diverse tasks that require common-sense **1482** reasoning. **1483 Visuomotor Instruction Tuning.** The concept of Visuomotor Instruction Tuning is purposed in **1484** LLaRA (Li et al., 2024b). This approach formulates robot policies as visuo-textual conversations and **1485** produces 2D keypoints and rotations for manipulation. However, it functions primarily as a high-level **1486** planner, and its outputs require additional adaptation before being directly executed on robots. **1487 1488** B.2 MULTI-STAGE TRAINING **1489 1490 OpenVLA-OFT.** OpenVLA-OFT (Kim et al., 2025) extends OpenVLA (Kim et al., 2024) by **1491** incorporating FiLM layers, Parallel decoding, MLP action head, and has been applied to fine-tuning on smaller simulation datasets such as LIBERO Liu et al. (2024b). This approach demonstrates **1492** the effectiveness of architectural enhancements for improving manipulation performance in specific **1493** domains. However, while these techniques improve in-domain performance, they fall short in **1494** reasoning-centric settings such as SimplerEnv-Instruct, as shown in Figure 6 (b). In contrast, our **1495** work moves beyond architectural modifications by emphasizing generalizable manipulation with **1496** textual reasoning through MoE adaptation, latent action methods, and a comprehensive data and **1497** evaluation pipeline. With the proposed VLA-IT training paradigm, our generalist model achieves **1498** nearly a 2 _×_ improvement over models that rely solely on architectural designs. **1499 Embodied Chain-of-Thought.** ECoT (Zawalski et al., 2024) introduces chain-of-thought (CoT) **1500** supervision to link reasoning with manipulation and follows a standard “pretrain-then-instruction-tune” **1501** paradigm. However, it relies on full-model pretraining fine-tuning, as in OpenVLA (Kim et al., 2024), **1502** which leads to catastrophic forgetting of vision-language capabilities. _In contrast, InstructVLA adopts_ **1503** _a two-stage design: the first stage injects action-generation ability while deliberately preserving_ **1504** _the multimodal knowledge of the pretrained VLM._ This approach ensures that the model retains **1505** open-world understanding and general multimodal reasoning, both of which are largely lost in ECoT. **1506** The second stage then strengthens multimodal reasoning and manipulation alignment. Consequently, **1507** InstructVLA supports broader inference modes (reasoning + manipulation, direct manipulation, and **1508** multimodal VQA) and achieves stronger performance with substantially fewer trainable parameters. **1509 Visual Chain-of-Thought.** CoT-VLA (Zhao et al., 2025) enhances manipulation by generating future **1510** image frames as visual chain-of-thought goals before predicting actions. While effective for goal **1511** specification, this approach relies on heavy video-generation supervision and does not exploit strong VLM pretraining for visual-language reasoning.

### I BROADER IMPACTS AND FUTURE WORK 

#### I.1 LIMITATION 

InstructVLA integrates world knowledge into manipulation tasks by performing multimodal reasoning prior to action generation. Recent VLMs also excel at long-context processing and multi-turn dialogue. This motivates curating interleaved manipulation and reasoning with multi-turn interaction to support long-horizon tasks involving user intervention or reasoning-action alternation (Yao et al., 2023). Furthermore, the existing tasks are limited to basic primitives such as open/close and pick/place due to the constraints of the datasets we use (Brohan et al., 2022; Ebert et al., 2021) and the capabilities of the simulator. In contrast, standard VLM benchmarks typically contain thousands of tasks. Extending InstructVLA and SimplerEnv-Instruct bench to more dexterous skills is essential for real-world deployment. 

#### I.2 LLM USAGE STATEMENT 

We employed large language models (LLMs) solely for grammar refinement and minor linguistic polishing. All LLM-assisted edits were carefully reviewed and verified by the authors to ensure that no fabricated content or unintended alterations to the original meaning were introduced. The research ideas, experimental design, data analysis, and conclusions presented in this work were entirely conceived and executed by the authors without LLM assistance. 

#### I.3 BROADER IMPACTS 

InstructVLA contributes to the advancement of general-purpose embodied agents by integrating vision-language understanding with action generation. Its ability to follow free-form instructions and generalize to novel tasks supports applications in assistive robotics and human-robot collaboration. Nonetheless, as with other large pretrained models, careful attention must be given to potential limitations such as dataset bias and safety in real-world deployment. Ensuring responsible use and reliable performance across diverse environments is essential. 

#### I.4 FUTURE WORK 

We plan to incorporate additional sensory modalities, such as depth and tactile feedback, to enhance safety and reliability in physical interactions. Leveraging recent advances in digital twins and simulation technologies, we aim to reduce reliance on real-world data by utilizing large-scale synthetic datasets. Finally, we will extend the evaluation and deployment of InstructVLA to a broader range of environments to further assess its generalization capabilities.
